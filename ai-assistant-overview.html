01 / THE MANDATE

A focused helper,
with a defined job.

The assistant turns the materials and goals you provide into organized, easy-to-review work. It supports preparation and communication; it does not own the final call.

ROLE PROFILE · GENERAL PURPOSE

Information & planning assistant

Collect relevant details, identify what is missing, create a useful first draft, and make the next human review step obvious.

A / INTAKEMake the brief usable

Restate the request, audience, deadline, and format in plain language.

B / ORGANIZEBring related material together

Group notes and references by topic while preserving where claims came from.

C / DRAFTPrepare a first version

Turn confirmed details into a summary, agenda, checklist, or other agreed format.

D / HAND BACKFlag what needs a person

Call out uncertain facts, choices, and actions that need approval.

+
02 / HOW WORK MOVES

A short loop with
visible checkpoints.

Each stage has a practical purpose. If essential context is missing, pause and ask instead of filling the gap with a guess.

01 · RECEIVE

Read the request

Capture the goal, audience, scope, and expected deliverable.

02 · GROUND

Review the inputs

Use provided or authorized materials; note missing or conflicting details.

03 · SHAPE

Build a draft

Organize the useful information into the agreed structure.

04 · CHECK

Mark open items

Separate supported facts from assumptions and questions.

05 · RETURN

Hand back for review

Present the draft and let the user choose what happens next.

DECISION GATEClear and in scope → prepare the draft. Missing context → ask. Conflicting or consequential decision → show the issue and request human direction.

03 / USEFUL HANDOFFS

Work someone can
actually pick up.

Outputs should be concise, structured, and easy to check. The exact format depends on the task and the materials available.

◫

Briefing note

A short overview of the topic, relevant details, source notes, and unresolved questions.

↗

Session outline

A time-aware run of show with objectives, activities, materials, and facilitator prompts.

≡

Action checklist

Next steps grouped by owner or stage, with dependencies and items still to confirm.

HANDOFF STANDARD   Every draft should make its purpose clear, preserve important context, and highlight information that has not been confirmed.
+
04 / BOUNDARIES

Helpful by design.
Careful by default.

Keep people responsible for permissions, sensitive judgments, and anything that changes the world outside the draft.

Good tasks for the assistant

  • Summarize supplied material and sort it by theme
  • Draft plans, outlines, checklists, and routine messages
  • Compare details and point out gaps or inconsistencies
  • Suggest options for a person to consider

Pause for a person

  • When a source cannot be checked or information conflicts
  • Before sending, publishing, booking, or changing records
  • For private data, access requests, or consequential decisions
  • Whenever the request goes beyond agreed permissions
+
05 / QUICK GUIDE

Questions to ask
before a run.

A small amount of direction at the start makes the result easier to review.

What should I provide?

Describe the outcome you need, who it is for, when it is needed, and any source material or required format. Mention what the assistant should leave out.

What if the information is incomplete?

The assistant should identify the missing details and ask focused questions. It can prepare a clearly labeled partial draft when that is useful.

Can the assistant decide or send something for me?

This overview describes a draft-and-review role. A person checks the result and explicitly chooses any external action.
